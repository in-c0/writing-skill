Results

One writing model, six briefs, four setups. Two blind AI editors scored every piece from 1–10. This is an early comparison, not a population-level result.

What happened when the judges stopped looking for AI tells?

The same 24 drafts were scored again with a neutral prompt: which would you publish, send, or keep reading? StoryScope still averaged 8.50, but the unassisted control climbed to 7.25. On short stories, the gap was just 8.00 vs 7.75, and the control won two of four first-place votes.

Install the skill

Three different approaches, not three required installs. Start with the one that fits what you're writing.

StoryScope: narrative fiction and story-driven nonfiction; draws on measured fiction patterns. The non-fiction guidance is a hypothesis, not a result of the original study.

writing-skill: clear documentation, explanations and editing that preserves an existing voice.

Combined: the reader-first rulebook with a StoryScope layer; an experimental option that did not consistently outperform the control here.

Uses the open skills.sh installer, which works with Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and other agents that read SKILL.md.

How this was measured

Six briefs: two journal posts, two pieces of sales copy, two short stories. Each brief was written four times by the same model in fresh sessions: no skill, writing-skill, writing-skill + StoryScope, and StoryScope alone. Two AI editors scored all four pieces per brief without knowing which was which. Stories were also annotated on the paper's 304 features and run through its released classifier.

This is a small, early benchmark: twelve judgements per setup, from judges in the writer's model family. The main rubric named AI tells that StoryScope was designed to avoid, which is why we also ran a neutral-rubric check. Self-selected human votes add perspectives but are not a representative sample. The original StoryScope study was on long-form fiction, not sales copy or journal posts, and its classifier was trained on ~5,000-word stories annotated by a different model. Its scores here are diagnostic comparisons, not authorship probabilities or writing-quality grades.

Research: Russell, Rajendhran, Pham, Iyyer, Wieting (2026), StoryScope (61,608 stories in the corpus; 61,575 rows in the released feature table). This repository's writing guidance and benchmark are an independent application of that research, not an evaluation or endorsement by the paper's authors.
