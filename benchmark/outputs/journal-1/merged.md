# One month in

I left my job on the last Friday of September. There was cake, and a card that eleven people signed, and someone wrote "Go build the next big thing!!" with two exclamation marks. I brought the card home and put it on my desk. It's still there, a bit curled now from sitting next to the window.

The plan was simple enough. I'd stop writing features for a product I didn't use and start making small apps of my own. Nothing huge. Little tools that each do one thing, that I could finish in a couple of weeks and put out into the world. I had enough savings for about eight months if I was careful, and a Notes file with forty-something ideas in it.

The first week felt great, mostly because it felt like a holiday. I slept in. I made proper coffee instead of drinking the office stuff. I set up a new repo, picked a stack, and spent a whole afternoon on an app icon, which in hindsight should have told me something.

The second week was when it got quiet. I hadn't realised how much of my day used to be other people. Standup at 9:30, someone asking if I'd looked at their PR yet, the lunch run where we complained about the same manager every time. I used to find all of that a bit annoying. Now I go whole days where the only person I talk to is the guy at the bottle shop, and that's only because he asks if I want a bag. I started taking my laptop to the library just to be near other people who were also pretending to work.

The work was slower too. At my old job, if I got stuck there was always someone to ask, or at least someone to complain to until the answer showed up. On my own, a bug with in-app purchases took me three days. Three days on a feature for an app that, it turned out, didn't need in-app purchases at all.

Still, I shipped two things. The first is a timer for boiling eggs. You pick how runny you want the yolk and it counts down. The second is a tiny habit tracker that lives in your menu bar and only tracks one habit, because I was annoyed that every other habit app wants you to track twelve.

The egg timer has been downloaded 23 times. At least four of those were me, testing on different devices, and two were my mum, who couldn't work out why it didn't install the first time. The habit tracker has 9 downloads and one review. The review says "ok" and gives it three stars. I've read it more times than I'd like to admit.

I still check App Store Connect before bed, as if the numbers might jump while I'm asleep. They don't.

The honest part is that I don't know yet if I made the right call. Some days I'm sure I did. I'll spend a morning on something that's fully mine, with nobody asking me to change the button colour, and it feels like the reason I learned to code in the first place. Other days I scroll past my old team's posts on LinkedIn, see them shipping things together, and feel like I stepped off a moving train to go and stand in a paddock by myself.

My savings went down by about the amount I expected, which is the only part of this month that went to plan. I've got a third app half-built, a small thing for saving recipes. I've also, quietly, updated my CV. I haven't sent it to anyone. I just wanted it to be ready.

Next month I'm going to try telling people about the apps somewhere other than my own account, where most of my followers are ex-coworkers and a bot that sells sunglasses. I'll write again at the end of November and say how that went.
