# One month in

It's been a month since my last day at work, so I'm writing down how it has actually gone before I start remembering it as better or worse than it was.

The short version is that I've shipped two small apps, hardly anyone has downloaded either of them, and I spend far more of each day alone than I thought I would.

I'd been planning this for most of a year. I had enough savings for about ten months, a notes file with around thirty app ideas in it, and a rough schedule: one small app every two weeks, see what gets traction, and put more time into whatever does. On paper it looked very sensible.

The first app was a timer for people who take short stretching breaks at their desk. It took eleven days instead of the five I'd given it. Most of that time wasn't spent on code. It went on App Store screenshots, a privacy policy, a rejection because my description mentioned a feature that wasn't in the build yet, and two days of redoing the settings screen because I didn't like how it looked. At my old job other people handled half of those things, and I never noticed how long they took.

It went live on a Tuesday. I posted it in two subreddits and on my own account, and by the end of the week it had 31 downloads. At least six of those were friends, because they messaged me to say they'd downloaded it.

The second app splits a grocery receipt between housemates. I built it in about eight days, mostly because I could reuse a lot from the first one. It has 14 downloads. One person left a review saying it crashed when they added more than ten items, and they were right. I fixed it that evening and then sat there wondering whether they would ever open it again.

The loneliness is the part I didn't plan for. I used to complain about standups and Slack pings. I didn't realise how much of my day they filled, or how much I relied on someone telling me "that looks good" or "that's broken" within an hour of pushing something. Now I can get from breakfast to dinner without saying a word out loud. Last week I caught myself talking to the kettle. I've started going to the library two days a week just to be around other people, even though I don't talk to any of them.

The slowness is harder to explain. Nothing is blocking me, and yet everything takes longer, because every decision is mine and nobody tells me when to stop polishing. I spent most of one morning choosing an icon colour.

So was it the right call? I don't know yet. Some days I'm sure it was. I like waking up and deciding what to work on, and I like that both apps exist, small as they are, because I decided they should. On other days I look at my bank balance, do the maths again, and think about how easy it would be to email a few old colleagues and ask if they're hiring.

A month isn't much to go on. I knew before I quit that most small apps get almost no downloads, and that the reason to make a lot of them is to find the one or two that do. I still check the download numbers more often than I'd like to admit, and it still bothers me when they don't move.

For month two I'm going to talk to people before I build anything, so the next app starts with at least one person who wants it, and spend less time on screenshots. I'm keeping the library days. I'll write another one of these at the end of the month, whatever the numbers look like.
