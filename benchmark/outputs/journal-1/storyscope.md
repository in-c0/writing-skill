# Month one: two apps, nineteen downloads

I quit on a Thursday in October. My manager said "living the dream" in a voice that suggested he'd be checking back, and someone from IT took my laptop and made me sign a sheet confirming I'd also returned the charger. I had. I'd bought my own the week before, along with a second monitor and a very good chair, because apparently the first thing I do when I lose a salary is spend money.

The plan was simple. Build small apps, ship them fast, see what sticks. I'd read the indie hacker threads. I knew the numbers were mostly bad. I figured I'd be one of the people they were bad for slowly, not quickly.

Here's what the month actually looked like.

In the first week I built Steep, a menu-bar timer for tea. You pick green, black or oolong and it pings you when it's done. I estimated two days. It took nine, mostly because I rewrote the settings screen three times and then lost an afternoon to a code-signing error that turned out to be one checkbox I hadn't ticked. When it finally went live I posted it to Hacker News and r/macapps. The HN post sat at one point, which is the point you get for posting it.

Steep has fourteen downloads. Four of them are my mum, who doesn't own a Mac and kept texting me screenshots of the App Store asking why she couldn't find it on her iPad.

The second app, Halves, splits a restaurant bill when some people didn't drink. I'm honestly proud of the logic. Apple rejected it twice: once for a missing privacy policy link, once because a screenshot showed a receipt with a real restaurant's name on it. It has five downloads. I know one of them is Dan from my old team, because he messaged "lol nice" and then, a day later, "wait how do I add tip".

That was the best part of my week.

Nobody tells you how quiet it gets. At work I complained about standups constantly. Now I'd pay to sit in one. I started going to the cafe near the station at the same time every morning so the guy behind the counter would say "the usual?" On day twenty he got my order wrong and I drank it anyway, because correcting him felt like too much conversation.

I also didn't expect to be this slow with nobody waiting on me. There's no ticket. Nobody asks in Slack whether it's merged yet. I'd sit down at nine with one clear task and by eleven I'd be reading the changelog for a framework I don't even use. Turns out deadlines were doing a lot of my work and never got credit for it.

The money is fine for now. I saved eight months of rent, which looked like a lot in the spreadsheet and looks like less every time I open my banking app.

People keep asking if it was the right call. My sister asked at dinner on Sunday and I said "too early to tell," which is true, and is also exactly what I'd say if the answer were no.

Next is a third app, ideally one for people who already exist and want something. I've opened a blank Xcode project four times this week and closed it again each time. Yesterday I put Steep on Product Hunt instead. It goes up on Tuesday. I've already muted the notifications.
