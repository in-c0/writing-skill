# Three Weeks Out

I strained my calf on a Tuesday, on a run I didn't even want to do.

It was meant to be an easy six kilometres. Four months into my training plan, easy sixes had become the filler between the runs that mattered, the ones I'd actually write about in my notes app afterwards. I was about two kilometres in, going up the slight hill past the bus depot, when something in my left calf went tight and then sharp, like someone had flicked it from the inside. I stopped and walked a few steps. Then I tried a slow jog, which was stupid, and it got worse. I walked home, about twenty-five minutes, with my watch still recording because I forgot to stop it. Strava has it logged as my slowest run ever.

Some background. Four months ago I signed up for my first half marathon. I'd never run further than about eight kilometres, and that was years ago. I found a sixteen-week beginner plan online, printed it, and stuck it on the fridge with a magnet from a pizza place. It was three runs a week at first, then four. Every Sunday was the long run, and every Sunday it got a little longer.

I liked it more than I expected to. Not every run, obviously. There were mornings when it was still dark and cold and I lay in bed bargaining with myself for ten minutes before getting up. But I liked the routine. I liked ticking off boxes on the fridge, and I liked eating a second breakfast without thinking about it. In week eleven I ran eighteen kilometres, the furthest I'd ever gone, and I was so pleased with myself that I texted my sister a screenshot of the map. She replied "why" and then sent a thumbs up.

If I'm honest, I also got a bit cocky. Around week twelve I started running my easy runs faster than the plan said, because easy pace felt embarrassing and I wanted the numbers to look good on Strava. I skipped the calf raises because the plan listed them as optional. I'm not saying that's definitely why this happened, and the physio didn't say that either. But I've thought about it.

The physio called it a grade one strain, which was a relief to hear. She pressed on a few spots and asked me to rate each one out of ten, and I kept saying four when it was more like six. I think part of me believed that if I gave her lower numbers she'd tell me I could run the race. She gave me a sheet of exercises and said no running for at least ten days, then a test jog, and then we'd see. When I asked about race day she said, "It's possible. Let's not plan around it yet."

So that's where I am. It's been eight days. I do my exercises twice a day on the living room floor while the kettle boils. I walk a lot. I've been using the exercise bike at the gym, which is the most boring forty minutes of my week, and I've got through most of a season of a baking show on my phone just to stay on it.

The pain is mostly gone now. What gets me is the fridge. The plan is still up there, with every box ticked until week thirteen and then nothing after that. I keep almost taking it down and then leaving it.

The race is in thirteen days. My bib arrived in the mail yesterday, and I put it on the bench next to the fruit bowl, where it still is. My test jog is on Saturday morning.
