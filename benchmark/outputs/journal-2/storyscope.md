# Three weeks out

I bought the shoes before I signed up for the race, which tells you something about how confident I was. Pegasus 40s, on sale, in a colour the website called "volt" and my housemate called "highlighter."

The plan was a free twelve-week one I found on Reddit, padded out to sixteen because I didn't trust myself. Four runs a week: intervals Tuesday, tempo Thursday, something easy Saturday, long run Sunday. I printed it and stuck it on the fridge under a magnet shaped like a pineapple, and for the first month the main thing I learned was that I hate Tuesdays.

Week one, my long run was 6 km and I walked twice. I told nobody. Week five I did 11 km around the Bay Run and texted my sister a screenshot of my watch, no message, just the screenshot. She replied "?? who are you." That was a good day.

Some of it was not fun and I'm not going to pretend it was. There was a stretch around week eight where every run felt like dragging a couch. I had a blister on my left heel that I named Gary. I started going to bed at 9:30 on Saturdays, which my friends took personally. One Sunday I got to 16 km and sat on the kerb outside a servo on Parramatta Road eating a Golden Gaytime at eight in the morning, and a guy filling up his ute gave me a thumbs up. I think about him a lot.

By week fourteen I'd run 19 km. My easy pace had dropped by almost a minute. I'd stopped checking the plan on the fridge because I knew it by heart. I had a goal time, which I'm not going to write here, because I'd already said it out loud to too many people.

Then, three weeks out, on a completely normal Thursday tempo, something in my right calf went *ping*. It wasn't dramatic. I didn't fall over. It felt like someone had flicked me with a rubber band from the inside, and the next step felt wrong, and the one after that felt worse, and I walked the 2 km home with my phone in my hand deciding whether to call someone. I didn't call anyone. I iced it with a bag of frozen peas and googled "calf strain how long" about forty times, which I don't recommend.

The physio was nice about it. Grade one, maybe borderline two. She pressed on the spot and I made a noise I didn't know I could make. "So, three weeks," she said, looking at the calendar on her screen, and then didn't finish the sentence, which I thought was a bit much.

What she actually said, when I pushed, was no running for at least ten days, then a walk-run test, then we'll see. Calf raises. Stationary bike if it doesn't hurt. She said some people come back fine for a race this close and some don't, and it depends on things neither of us knows yet. I nodded like that was useful information.

It's been six days. I do my calf raises at the kitchen bench while the kettle boils. I rode the bike at the gym next to a woman watching *Married at First Sight* on her phone with no headphones in, and honestly it helped. The race confirmation email is still in my inbox, and so is the one about deferring to next year, which you have to do by Friday.

Yesterday I walked to the shops without limping for the first time, and on the way back I caught myself doing the maths. Ten days off, then the test, then maybe ten days before the race. Maybe.

My sister texted last night asking if I'm still running it. I said I'd know by Friday. I won't, really. Friday's just when I have to pick.
