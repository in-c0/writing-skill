# Three Weeks Out

I've been putting off this post for a week. Here's what happened. I strained my left calf on a Tuesday morning run, three weeks before the half marathon I'd been training for since July.

Let me back up a bit.

In July I printed a sixteen-week plan off a running site and stuck it on the fridge with a magnet from a pizza place. Before that, the longest I'd ever run was about eight kilometres, and that was years ago. The plan started gently: three runs a week, a couple of short ones and one longer one on Sunday. The first Sunday "long run" was six kilometres, and I remember feeling a bit smug about how easy it was.

It got harder slowly enough that I mostly didn't notice. By September I was running 14 km on Sundays and actually looking forward to it. I'd found a loop that goes past the bakery twice, which helped. I also learned that I need to eat something before any run over an hour, that cheap socks give me a blister on my second toe, and that if I start too fast I pay for it around the 10 km mark every single time. I crossed off each session on the fridge plan with a red pen, and by mid-October the sheet was mostly red.

The 18 km run the weekend before I got hurt was the best I've ever felt running. When I finished I texted my sister a photo of my watch, which she ignored, fairly.

Then came the Tuesday. It was an easy 6 km recovery run, the kind I'd done dozens of times by then. About two kilometres in, going up a small hill, I felt something in my calf grab. It wasn't dramatic. It felt like someone had flicked the muscle from the inside. I stopped, walked for a minute, tried to jog again, and it hurt enough that I walked the rest of the way home.

I saw a physio on Thursday. She pressed on a few spots and had me do single-leg calf raises. I managed four on the bad side and about twenty-five on the good side. She called it a grade one strain, which is the mild kind, and said "two to three weeks" and then "we'll see", which is not quite the same as yes.

So now I do calf raises off the bottom stair while the kettle boils, and I ride the stationary bike at the gym, which I find very boring. Short walks are fine. I'm not allowed to run until the raises stop hurting, and I test this about six times a day.

The hardest part has been watching the plan on the fridge change from a record of what I'd done into a list of sessions I'm missing. I keep doing the maths. If I can run again by next weekend, I'd have one week before the race. That's probably enough to finish if I take it easy. It isn't enough to run the time I'd quietly been hoping for, which was under two hours. I hadn't told anyone that number, and it feels a bit silly writing it here now.

The physio and I have talked through what happens next. If my calf feels normal by race week, I'll start, run slower than I'd planned, and walk if it tightens up. If it doesn't, I'll defer my entry to next year. The race allows that for a small fee. I checked the same night I got hurt, before I'd even taken my shoes off.

Either way, I can run 18 km now, and in June I couldn't run eight. I'm trying to remember that when I look at the fridge.

My next physio appointment is on the 14th, and I'll post an update after that. For now, back to the stairs.
