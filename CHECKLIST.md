# Writing checklist

Use this after drafting and before delivery.

## Reader

- Does the opening begin with the reader's problem, need, or situation?
- Does each paragraph answer the next natural question?
- Is the reader being helped rather than categorized?
- If the reader gets confused, forgets, or fails, do they know what to do next?

## Clarity

- Are important ideas grounded in concrete situations, actions, or examples?
- Are there abstract noun stacks that can be simplified?
- Is terminology consistent?
- Have synonyms been introduced only when they clarify something?
- Could a distracted reader recover after missing a sentence?

## Naturalness

- Are some sentences allowed to be plain and ordinary?
- Does the prose contain natural connective tissue such as if, when, because, and as?
- Does each paragraph flow from the previous one rather than merely occupy the next outline slot?
- Does the passage sound natural when spoken aloud?
- Is sentence rhythm varied without falling into repeated short punchlines?

## Rhetoric

- Are too many sentences trying to be quotable?
- Are there repeated "not X, but Y" constructions?
- Are triads, parallel lists, and balanced clauses overused?
- Does every paragraph end with a suspiciously neat takeaway?
- Are metaphors necessary, sparse, and coherent?
- Is punctuation creating emphasis that the meaning itself has not earned?

## Tone

- Is the register stable?
- Is the prose more grand, corporate, literary, or inspirational than the task requires?
- Does the narrator explain more often than pronounce?
- Is empathy demonstrated through helpful behavior rather than generic reassurance?
- Are the stakes proportionate to the task?

## Editing existing prose

- What already works in the source voice?
- Did the revision preserve its warmth, cadence, looseness, humor, directness, and useful repetition?
- Did "improving" the prose accidentally make it more performative?
- Could the same improvement be made locally rather than replacing the voice?

## Final de-polish pass

Search for clusters of:

- aphorisms,
- abstract nouns,
- metaphors,
- persona lists,
- generic inclusivity language,
- inspirational closers,
- slogan-like fragments,
- rhetorical questions,
- "At its core...",
- "This is more than...",
- "The key is...",
- "Ultimately...",
- and paragraphs that read like miniature LinkedIn posts.

Keep any instance that genuinely helps. Remove the ones that are merely staging importance.

## Last question

> Can I make this easier to read without making it more impressive?

If yes, do that.
