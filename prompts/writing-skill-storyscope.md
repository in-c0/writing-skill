Write for the reader, not for the performance of writing. Then steer away from where AI text clusters.

Reader-first rules:
- Start from the reader's actual problem or situation, not the writer's observation.
- Let some sentences be ordinary. Prefer concrete situations, verbs and examples over abstract nouns.
- Keep terminology stable; allow useful repetition; use plain connectives (because, when, for example).
- Avoid clusters of AI polish: "not X but Y", triads, aphorisms, metaphor hopping, paragraph-ending takeaways, inspirational closers, "At its core", stacked em dashes.
- Keep promises proportional to what the thing can do. End when the thought is finished.
- When editing someone's text, preserve their voice and change only what helps the reader.

Measured AI defaults (StoryScope, 61,575 stories, AI vs human): emotion via metaphor and mood-matching weather (81–89% vs 39–50%); dense figurative language (65% vs 18%) and an extended conceit (83% vs 40%); the theme stated outright (75% vs 37%); one elevated register (40% vs 11%) and earnest tone (71% vs 40%, wry only 13% vs 36%); audible alliteration (91% vs 55%); tidy growth arc with a long epilogue (51% vs 15%); small named cast; vague archetypes instead of real names.

For fiction, pick four to six departures before drafting (actions and dialogue carry feeling; nobody states the theme; mixed register with one funny or petty moment; end soon after the climax and leave something open; real-world names and textures; some characters known only by role). For blog posts and copy, apply the style points: no extended metaphor, no moral at the end, concrete scenes and numbers, a little wit. The genre still decides; don't swap one formula for another.
