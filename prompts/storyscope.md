Before you write, know where AI writing goes by default. A study of 61,575 stories (StoryScope, Russell et al. 2026: human authors vs GPT, Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek and Kimi on the same prompts) found AI text converges on the same choices. Share of AI stories vs human stories:

- Emotion shown through metaphor, body sensations and weather that mirrors mood (81–89% vs 39–50%), instead of through what people do and say.
- Dense figurative language (65% vs 18%), a sustained extended metaphor (83% vs 40%), a recurring image motif (96% vs 69%).
- The theme or lesson stated outright (75% vs 37%); a narrator explaining what it all means (76% vs 52%).
- One unbroken elevated register (40% vs 11%); earnest or lyrical tone (71% vs 40%), rarely wry (13% vs 36%).
- Noticeable alliteration and rhythm (91% vs 55%); list-like parallel sentences (99% vs 70%).
- Tidy arc: the protagonist grows, resolves things by choice or acceptance, and the piece lingers after the climax (51% vs 15%).
- Small, fully named cast introduced by description; vague archetypes instead of real songs, brands, places.

Any one of these is fine. Hitting most of them at once is what makes text read as AI.

So, for this piece:
1. Pick four to six deliberate departures that suit the brief, for example: carry feeling through actions and dialogue; keep figurative images few and let one be a stock phrase a person would really think; never state the theme or end on a lesson; mix registers and let someone be funny, petty or wrong once; end soon after the turning point and leave something open; use real-world names and textures; for stories, a slightly larger cast with some people known only by role.
2. Write it.
3. Reread once for clusters (three paragraphs in a row ending on an image, a closing line that restates the point) and fix only those spots.

For sales copy, essays and blog posts, apply the style points only: no extended metaphor, no line announcing what it all "really" means, no alliterative triple slogans, concrete scenes and numbers over imagery, a little wit over unbroken earnestness. Keep the genre in charge: a fable can keep its moral. The aim is to leave the default, not to adopt a new formula.
