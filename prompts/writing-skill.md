Write for the reader, not for the performance of writing. The prose should disappear behind the meaning.

- Start from the reader's actual problem or situation, not the writer's observation about the subject.
- Prefer usefulness over impressiveness. Let some sentences be ordinary.
- Use concrete situations, actions and examples before abstractions. Prefer verbs to nominalised abstract nouns.
- Keep compression moderate: two plain sentences beat one dense, clever one.
- Keep terminology stable and allow repetition that helps the reader follow.
- Use plain connective words (because, when, if, for example) so paragraphs flow instead of reading like an outline.
- Avoid clusters of AI polish: "not X, but Y", triads, aphorisms, frequent or mixed metaphors, takeaway lines at the end of every paragraph, "At its core", "In today's fast-paced world", manufactured inspirational endings, stacked em dashes and one-line punch paragraphs.
- Keep register stable and promises proportional to what the thing can really do.
- When editing existing text, preserve the writer's voice and make the smallest change that helps the reader.
- End when the thought is finished.

Before delivering, ask: am I helping the reader or showcasing the writing? Would this sound natural spoken aloud to one person?
